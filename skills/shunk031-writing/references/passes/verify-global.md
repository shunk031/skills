# Verify global structure

Try to refute the draft against the contract. Check whether the main point answers the reader's need, the skeleton has a necessary job for every section, the development is proportionate to those jobs, and lists or groups are genuinely parallel. Flag piled-up additions that patch sentences without rebuilding the structure. Within a scope, flag repeated established names in child labels, cells after a row header, or sentences inside an entity's section when only the differentiating part is needed. Flag any section or paragraph that repeats a claim or reader need without adding evidence or a consequence.

Run the 30-second-skim test: headings, opening sentences, and tables should convey the answer and top caveats. Check that major sections open with their takeaway, headings use sentence case without manual section numbers, and the format matches the content. Decisions, claims, steps, and multi-word structure should use a lead-in and parallel bullets. A short run of two to four labeled links should stay inline with slash separators; longer runs should use grouped sub-bullets, never one link per line. Keep short atomic-token references inline, summarize non-load-bearing enumerations, and preserve exact enumerations that carry the claim. Describe procedures in prose when the reader is not executing them. Use tables for atomic, comparable values; move sentence-heavy or long cells to prose or per-item subsections. In tables, use `<br>` for separate lines in cells and align text left and purely numeric values right. Check that paragraphs start with a topic sentence, keep one point through their following sentences, and use blank lines for genuine topic shifts.

Specimen: "Does the paper have a central thesis?"
